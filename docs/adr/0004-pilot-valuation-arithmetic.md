# ADR 0004: integer valuation and private tier arithmetic

Status: implemented as development subcircuits; composed profile activation pending.

Transfer records permit unsigned 128-bit base-unit quantity, rational valuation
numerator/denominator, and USD-cent result. Multiplying two such integers can
require 256 bits, exceeding the BN254 scalar field. A direct circuit equation
`quantity * numerator = cents * denominator + remainder` is therefore insufficient:
it establishes equality modulo the field, not integer equality.

`PilotMultiply128` splits each operand into two 64-bit limbs and computes all four
partial products. Products are at most 128 bits; intermediate sums are at most
129 bits. Low limbs are range-constrained to 64 bits, and the middle carry is
bounded to 65 bits. Every witness hint has an explicit reconstruction equality
and range constraint. The output is four 64-bit limbs for the integer product.

`PilotValuation` separately computes quantity×numerator and cents×denominator,
then adds a 128-bit remainder using constrained binary carries. All four limbs
must agree and the final carry must be zero. Quantity, numerator, denominator and
cents are nonzero; remainder must be less than denominator. These conditions
establish cents = floor(quantity×numerator/denominator) without field wrapping.
The Python witness builder uses arbitrary-precision integer `divmod`, never float.

The adversarial fixture chooses quantity = floor(p/2^128)+1 and numerator =
2^128−1, where p is the BN254 scalar modulus. The false quotient
quantity×numerator−p fits in 128 bits and satisfies the naive field equation with
denominator one and remainder zero, despite differing from the true integer
product. Actual WASM witness generation rejects it under the limb constraints.
Other fixtures exercise carry boundaries, products near 2^256, wrong quotients,
invalid remainders (including an equation-preserving oversized remainder), zero
operands and out-of-range inputs.

`PilotAmountTier` uses positive ordered 128-bit USD-cent thresholds and derives
tier = 1 + the number of thresholds met. Equality is inclusive at each threshold.
Tier is an input constrained to that result, with no output for SAR or tier.
The composed profile must keep this input private; omission of an output in this
subcircuit alone does not establish privacy for an eventual public interface.
The legacy amount circuit and its public SAR signal remain unchanged until the
coordinated proof-version migration.

These predicates do not authenticate valuation provenance, enforce rational
reduction, select jurisdictional policy, or prove all legal obligations. Canonical
transfer validation enforces reduced ratios. The composed circuit must bind the
quantity, ratio, amount and thresholds to the validated transfer and approved
valuation/policy commitments. A caller-selected ratio or policy is not trusted.
No proving keys or production artifacts are generated by these witness tests.

# Pricing discovery experiment

These are internal hypotheses, not market prices, an offer or permission to send
quotes. No customer has accepted these amounts. Validate job value and delivery
cost before selecting an actual price.

## Scope held constant

Use the bounded pilot brief: one tenant, one read-only source, one workflow,
one policy and one export/review process over a proposed six-week measurement
window. Estimate integration/support effort before attaching a price. Hosting,
retention period, source fees, security work and support hours must be explicit;
never promise unlimited services or an SLA that has not been costed and staffed.

## Hypothetical price cards

Discuss the same scoped pilot at illustrative fixed fees of **$3,000, $6,000 and
$12,000**. Present them transparently as research scenarios, rotate presentation
order, and ask what budget/process or scope would change at each level. Record
objections and actual next actions, not only a yes/no willingness-to-pay answer.
These amounts are deliberately test inputs, not a researched market benchmark.

For continuation, test whether the buyer prefers a predictable managed-service
subscription or a smaller base with a capped usage allowance. Illustrative monthly
cards of **$1,000, $2,500 and $5,000** are separate research inputs. Do not combine
pilot and recurring responses into a forecast or claim conversion probability.

Proposed chargeable value is managed connectors, reviewed policy history,
reconciliation integration, support and encrypted evidence preservation. Keep
verification, portable formats and the local simulator in the free distribution
plan. Avoid charging by ALLOW outcome or making export/independent verification a
paid-only escape route.

## Cost and buying evidence

For each prospect alias, record the accepted job, current cost, named budget owner,
price card/order, requested scope, procurement constraints, explicit next action
and date. Separately record estimated and actual engineer/support hours, compute,
retention/backup and upstream fees. Calculate contribution as revenue minus these
direct costs, with the costing assumptions visible. Missing cost data blocks a
margin claim.

Advance to an actual proposal only when the budget owner, data permissions,
measurable scope and delivery resources are identified. Owner review selects one
concrete scope and price; do not imply randomized hypothetical prices are binding
quotes. F4 customer access/agreement remains required. Record a signed engagement
separately from an interview response or letter of interest.

## Decision rules

After an initial exploratory set of 5–8 qualified conversations, review evidence
qualitatively; this sample is not a statistical pricing study. Continue only if
multiple prospects identify the same costly job, a credible buying path exists,
and scoped delivery cost can support the chosen price. Revise when value exists
but integration/support dominates cost. Stop the proposed offer when the incumbent
workflow solves the job adequately or no owner will allocate time/budget.

Usage allowances are a later contract decision. The implemented inventory is not
an invoice ledger: retries do not add retained rows, new logical observations can,
and retention changes may reduce inventory. Any metered invoice needs an agreed
unit, interval, immutable billing events, corrections/credits and reconciliation;
no payment processor or billing automation is implemented by this packet.
